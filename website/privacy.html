Privacy Policy

Last updated October 22, 2026

NutriFacts reads the nutrition facts and ingredients on Japanese food packaging and explains them in English. It has no accounts, no ads and no analytics or tracking. This page explains the one thing it does send off your iPhone: the photo of a label, so it can be read.

What stays on your iPhone

  • The camera view. While you point at a pack, the app looks for the nutrition label in the camera view on your iPhone. None of the live view is sent anywhere.
  • Your scan history. Scans, their photos and what was read from them are saved only on your iPhone. Delete one by swiping it in History, or all of them with Clear History. Deleting the app deletes them too.
  • Your settings, such as the health guidelines you chose.
  • Exports. Copying a food or exporting your history as CSV hands the data to the app you choose. NutriFacts doesn't send it anywhere itself.

What's sent to read a label

When you take a photo of a label (or pick one from your library), that photo is sent to be read. Nothing else is sent with it: no name, account or device identifier. Which service reads it depends on Settings › Read Labels With:

  • ai& (the default). The photo goes to the NutriFacts server, which runs on Cloudflare and passes it to an AI model run by AI& to read the label. The NutriFacts server doesn't store the photo or what was read from it; it sends the reading back to your iPhone. Cloudflare keeps standard request logs (such as the time and IP address of a request) for a short time so we can find faults and stop abuse. [AI&’s retention: to confirm before launch.]
  • Apple Intelligence. The photo is read by Apple's on-device model, or by Apple's Private Cloud Compute, which Apple designs so that your data isn't stored or made available to Apple. See Apple's privacy policy.

Photos of labels usually show only packaging. Try not to include people or personal documents in the frame.

The beta sign-up

If you join the beta on this website, your email address is stored by our email provider, Kit, and used only to send you the TestFlight link and beta news. Every email has an unsubscribe link.

Children

NutriFacts isn't directed at children under 13 and doesn't knowingly collect their information.

Not medical advice

NutriFacts shows what a label prints, with daily values and levels from the health guidelines you choose. It isn't medical or dietary advice, and a reading can be wrong. Check the result against the label photo, and talk to a doctor or dietitian about your diet, allergies or a medical condition.
