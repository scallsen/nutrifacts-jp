Field guide · Japanese food labels

栄養成分表示Reading a Japanese nutrition label

Japanese packs print five numbers in a fixed order, with no daily values. The confusing part is the carbohydrate lines. They nest inside each other, and whether a line is printed tells you as much as the number on it. This page shows how the label works and what a simplified view of it can say.

The label

What every panel contains

This is the full format, with every optional line a manufacturer might add. Most products only print the five mandatory rows.

  1. A
    The heading and the unit

    栄養成分表示 is always the title. Next to it is the amount the numbers refer to: 100g当たり, 100ml当たり, one serving (with its weight), one pack or one piece. The manufacturer picks it. There's no standard serving size.

  2. B
    Five mandatory rows, always in this order

    Energy (熱量, kcal), protein (たんぱく質), fat (脂質), carbohydrate (炭水化物), salt equivalent (食塩相当量). The order is fixed by law.

  3. C
    Indented rows mean “of which”

    A dash or indent marks a part of the row above. Saturated fat is part of fat. Under carbohydrate, 糖質 and 食物繊維 are its two parts, and 糖類 is part of 糖質.

  4. D
    Salt, not sodium

    Japan prints grams of salt (NaCl). To get sodium in mg, multiply by 393.7. A sodium line only appears on foods with no added sodium salts.

  5. E
    推定値 means estimate

    The values were calculated from ingredient tables or a sample, not lab-tested for this product. That is legal and very common. The usual ±20% accuracy rule doesn't apply.

The confusing part

How the carbohydrate lines nest

The three carb terms aren't alternatives. Each one sits inside the next. The bar uses the granola from above: 36.0 g of carbohydrate per 50 g serving.

糖質 and 食物繊維 come as a pair

If a label shows either one, it has to show both. If you see net carbs, fibre is on the label too.

糖類 can appear on its own

A label can list sugars straight under carbohydrate without splitting out fibre. Then you know the sugar, but the rest is an unknown mix of starch and fibre.

Usually there's just 炭水化物

Fibre is only “recommended”, so most packs skip the breakdown. Then you can't calculate net carbs or sugar. You only know the upper limit.

“Low-carb” products split 糖質 from 糖類

Sugar alcohols such as erythritol and maltitol count as 糖質 but not 糖類. A bar can show 6 g of 糖質 and under 1 g of sugar.

    Daily reference values

    Labels don't print %DV, but these official 2025 values (adults, 2,200 kcal) let you calculate it.

    sodium mg = salt g × 1000 ÷ 2.54 kcal ≈ protein×4 + fat×9 + 糖質×4 + fibre×2 (no fibre listed: + 炭水化物×4) 糖類 ≤ 糖質 ≤ 炭水化物 糖質 + 食物繊維 = 炭水化物
